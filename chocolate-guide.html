Chapter One

Working With Chocolate

Why Tempering Matters

Good temper gives chocolate its snap, gloss, and smooth melt. Warm it fully, cool it down to re-form stable crystals, then gently reheat to a workable temperature — never rush the curve.

Melting

  • Melt over a double boiler, or in short microwave bursts, stirring between each one.
  • Keep every bowl and tool completely dry — even a drop of water can seize the batch.
  • Work in a cool, dry room, away from direct sun and strong kitchen odors.

Avoiding Bloom

Chalky white streaks usually mean poor temper or a temperature swing during storage. Store finished pieces somewhere cool and steady, never in the fridge unless the box says otherwise.

Dipping

Dip in one smooth motion, let the excess drip back into the bowl, then set on parchment to firm up undisturbed.

Piping & Drizzle

Keep a steady, close hand and a light, even pressure for a clean, consistent line.

Chapter Two

Talking With Clients

Know Your Craft

  • Learn the flavors, fillings, and cacao percentages we carry.
  • Know which items are made fresh daily versus made to order.
  • Remember returning clients — knowing their taste builds trust.

Understand the Order

  • Ask about the occasion, the recipient, and any allergies before quoting.
  • Confirm quantity and packaging style — box, bouquet, or tiered display.
  • Fill out the order form completely before the client leaves.

How We Speak

We don't say "no" — we say "let me check and get back to you." Keep the tone warm, keep the workspace quiet when working on-site, and speak English with clients so nothing gets lost.
