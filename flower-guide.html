Chapter One

Caring for Fresh Flowers

Before You Begin

  • Check every delivery for damage or leakage before it leaves the cold room.
  • Keep pots and vases clean — rinse and dry them before each use.
  • Let flowers rest and adjust to room temperature before working with them.

Conditioning

  1. Remove all petals, leaves, or thorns that would sit below the waterline.
  2. Cut each stem at a slant, ideally underwater, so air doesn't block water flow.
  3. Place stems immediately into a clean bucket of fresh water.
  4. Give softer varieties time to fully absorb water before arranging.

Helping Tired Stems Recover

If a flower has gone soft, re-cut the stem underwater, wrap it snugly in paper for support, and let it stand in cool water for a few hours — most will firm back up.

Finishing Touches

  • Use wire, tape, or a bamboo pick to support a delicate or bent stem.
  • Tuck moss, paper, or cellophane around the base of an arrangement to hide the foam and hold moisture in.

Chapter Two

Talking With Clients

Know Your Craft

  • Learn the names, prices, and origins of the flowers we carry.
  • Know which blooms are in season, so you can suggest freely.
  • Remember returning clients — knowing their taste builds trust.

Understand the Order

  • Ask about the occasion, the recipient, and where the piece will be placed — it shapes the size and style.
  • Fill out the order form completely before the client leaves.
  • If unsure about a request, check with a colleague or another branch rather than guessing.

How We Speak

We don't say "no" — we say "let me check and get back to you." Keep the tone warm, keep the workspace quiet when working on-site, and speak English with clients so nothing gets lost.
